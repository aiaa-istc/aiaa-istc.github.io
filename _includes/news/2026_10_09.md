**Abstract:** This talk presents research on control and optimization for unmanned aircraft systems (UAS), with an emphasis on enabling safe, efficient, and intelligent autonomous operations. The talk will first discuss distributed formation control and collision avoidance for multi-UAV systems, including optimal control strategies for coordinated flight and safe separation. Then, it will introduce physics-informed learning approaches that integrate model predictive control, system dynamics, and stability considerations to enable efficient quadcopter control. The talk will also explore system-level optimization for Advanced Air Mobility, including vehicle assignment, routing, and coordination between ground and air transportation. Overall, these studies demonstrate how control theory, mathematical optimization, and artificial intelligence can be integrated to support the development of next-generation UAS.

**Bio:** Dr. Shuting Yang is an Assistant Professor of Aerospace Science Engineering at Tuskegee University. She received her B.S., M.S., and Ph.D. in Aeronautics and Astronautics Engineering from Purdue University. Her research focuses on advanced air mobility, unmanned aerial systems, flight dynamics and control, mathematical optimization, and artificial intelligence. Her work spans system-level modeling and optimization of multimodal transportation networks, distributed control and collision avoidance for multi-UAV systems, and physics-informed and AI-assisted control methods for autonomous flight. Her current research seeks to bridge optimization, control theory, machine learning, and experimental flight testing to develop safe and intelligent aerospace systems while expanding hands-on research opportunities for students.
